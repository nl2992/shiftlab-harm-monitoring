\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:limits}

Paired disagreement gating pays when disagreements are rare relative to the stream and the budget can reach them, and its gain can be anticipated before deployment
from the disagreement rate, the label budget and the two support bounds. The results suggest three choices for an operator. Gate on disagreement before choosing a betting engine,
since every gated arm outperforms every ungated one at scarce budgets on localised harm. Use a fixed gating rate when the aim is the earliest alarm, and a paced rate, with its floor
set in advance, when the monitor must keep running through a burst of disagreement. Provision the label budget from the detectability map for the interventions that matter.

\paragraph{Scope and next steps.}

The synthetic streams use imposed costs and a single deployed-versus-reference pair per scenario, and the EIA paths are previously examined development windows
with costs imposed on a forecasting task. A prospective, operator-costed deployment is the next test. The detectability map locates the useful region under a strong intervention above a $5\%$ budget;
extending it to scarcer budgets is a natural direction, through more informative signals than the paired cost alone.
